Crystal Growing Semester Project

Monopotassium Phosphate (MKP) — From Solution to Structure

CHM-130 · Fundamentals of Chemistry · 5-Week Project
Introduction

Today you will prepare a supersaturated solution and set a crystal in motion. Over the next 5 weeks, that crystal will grow — atom by atom, layer by layer — into a structure you can hold in your hand. Along the way, you will see solubility, supersaturation, nucleation, and crystal habit stop being vocabulary words and become things you can watch happen in real time.

The compound you will grow is monopotassium phosphate (MKP), chemical formula KH2PO4. It is a water-soluble fertilizer and — in its purest form, known as KDP — the same material used in high-power laser optics, chosen there precisely because it grows into exceptionally clear, well-ordered crystals. It dissolves readily in warm water and crystallizes into tetragonal prisms with pointed pyramidal ends as the solution cools — the same supersaturation mechanism that forms kidney stones in the human body.

🏥 Clinical Connection — Why Nurses Should Understand Crystallization

  • Kidney stones (nephrolithiasis): Calcium oxalate and calcium phosphate crystals form in urine when concentration exceeds solubility — the identical supersaturation mechanism you will observe in this lab.
  • Gout: Monosodium urate crystallizes in synovial fluid of joints when serum uric acid exceeds ~6.8 mg/dL (its solubility limit at 37 °C).
  • Gallstones: Cholesterol crystallization in bile — supersaturation plus nucleation on mucin glycoproteins.
  • Drug polymorphism: The same drug molecule can form different crystal structures (polymorphs) with different dissolution rates. Ritonavir's market recall in 1998 happened because an unexpected polymorph formed during manufacturing, rendering the capsules therapeutically inactive.
  • IV crystalloid fluids: The clinical term "crystalloid" (Normal Saline, Lactated Ringer's) literally derives from substances that can form crystals — contrasted with "colloids" that cannot.
Key Concepts

Solubility describes the maximum amount of a solute that will dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature. For MKP, solubility increases steadily with temperature:

Temperature (°C)Solubility (g MKP per 100 g H₂O)What It Means
1018Cold tap water
2023Room temperature — your lab
3028Warm (growing-solution target)
5043Warm water tap (refeed prep)
7064Hot (seed-solution prep)
8077Very hot — handle with care
100106Boiling point of water

Supersaturation occurs when a hot, concentrated solution cools: it now contains more dissolved solute than the solvent can hold at the lower temperature. The excess must come out of solution as solid crystals. The degree of supersaturation controls what happens next:

  • High supersaturation → many tiny crystals (rapid nucleation, small crystal size)
  • Low supersaturation → fewer, larger, higher-quality crystals (slow growth, better structure)

In Week 1 you will use high supersaturation to generate many small seed crystals. In Week 2 you will select the best seed and move it into a low-supersaturation environment where it can grow slowly into a large, well-formed crystal over the remaining three weeks.

Nucleation is the event where dissolved molecules first organize into a tiny ordered cluster — the "birth" of a crystal. Nucleation can be homogeneous (spontaneous) or heterogeneous (triggered by a surface — a scratch on glass, a dust particle, or a seed crystal you deliberately introduce).

Crystal system: MKP crystallizes in the tetragonal system (a = b ≠ c, all angles 90°). Expect elongated prismatic crystals with pointed pyramidal tips — the classic "little pencil" or "obelisk" shape. Because MKP grows cleanly, your final crystal should be optically clear enough to see straight through.

  • ~90–100 g per student total across 5 weeks: 40 g for seed prep (Week 1), 30 g for growing solution (Week 2), two ~10 g refeeds (Weeks 3 & 4)

  • 16 oz wide-mouth mason jar (your personal growing vessel)
  • 250 mL glass beaker (shared, for weighing & dissolving)
  • Glass stir rod
  • Thermometer (0–110 °C) — shared
  • Hot plate or electric kettle — shared, ≥ 80 °C
For Crystal Suspension
  • Clear monofilament fishing line (2–4 lb test)
  • Wooden popsicle stick or pencil (to bridge jar opening)
  • Coffee filter + rubber band (jar cover)
  • Masking tape & permanent marker (labeling)
For Measurement & Safety
  • Ruler with mm divisions
  • Digital scale (± 0.1 g) — shared
  • Safety goggles (required)
  • Lab apron or old clothes
  • Paper towels

⚠️ Safety Precautions

  • Goggles required whenever handling MKP solution or hot water.
  • MKP solutions are mildly acidic (pH ≈ 4.2–4.7) — avoid eye contact. In case of splash, flush with water for 15 minutes and notify your instructor.
  • Hot water hazard: Seed solutions are prepared at ≥ 80 °C. Handle beakers with heat-resistant gloves or beaker tongs. Never carry open beakers of hot solution across the room.
  • MKP is low-toxicity (used as a food-grade fertilizer and hydroponic plant food), but do not ingest. Wash hands thoroughly after each session.
  • Disposal: MKP solution can be poured down the drain with running water — it is not a hazardous waste. Leftover solid MKP can go in the regular trash.
  • If crystals break, the fragments are not sharp in the way glass is, but dispose of small pieces rather than leaving them loose.

Week 1 — Solution Preparation & Nucleation (First Session) ▶

Goal: Dissolve MKP in hot water to create a strongly supersaturated solution. As it cools undisturbed over the next 2–5 days, many small seed crystals will form.

💡 The Chemistry

At 80 °C, water can dissolve ~77 g of MKP per 100 g. At room temperature (~20 °C), it can only hold ~23 g. By dissolving 40 g in 100 mL of hot water, then cooling, you force ~17 g to crystallize out. That mass becomes hundreds of tiny seed crystals coating the bottom and sides of your jar.

  1. Put on safety goggles. Label your mason jar with your name, section, and the date using masking tape.
  2. Using the digital scale, weigh out 40 g of MKP powder. Record the exact mass in your data table.
  3. Measure 100 mL of water in your beaker. Heat on a hot plate (or use pre-heated water from the instructor) until the temperature reaches 80 °C ± 5 °C. Record the temperature.
  4. Slowly add the 40 g of MKP to the hot water while stirring continuously with a glass rod. Stir for at least 2 minutes until all powder is completely dissolved. The solution should be perfectly clear — no visible grains. If any powder remains, continue heating gently and stirring.
  5. Carefully pour the clear hot solution into your labeled mason jar.
  6. Do not stir once poured. Allow the solution to cool undisturbed.
  7. Cover the jar loosely with a coffee filter secured with a rubber band (keeps out dust, allows slow evaporation).
  8. Place the jar in your assigned shelf location. Do not move it until Week 2.
  9. In your observation log, sketch the solution and record: initial mass of MKP, volume of water, dissolution temperature, and the time you set it to cool.

Week 2 — Seed Crystal Selection & Growth Solution Setup ▶

Goal: Choose the best seed crystal from Week 1's crop. Prepare a fresh, mildly supersaturated growing solution. Suspend the seed in the new solution to begin controlled growth.

  1. Observe your jar. You should see a mass of small crystals on the bottom and possibly the sides. Record your observations and sketch what you see.
  2. Carefully pour the remaining liquid (the "mother liquor") into a separate container and set it aside — do not discard it. It will go back in after you add the seed.
  3. Select your seed crystal. Examine the crop and choose the single best specimen:
    • Look for a crystal that is clear (not cloudy or milky)
    • Look for well-defined faces and edges (symmetrical pencil or obelisk shape)
    • Choose one that is approximately 3–5 mm in its longest dimension
    • Avoid crystals that are fused together in clusters
  4. Tie or loop a piece of monofilament fishing line around your seed crystal. If it is too small to tie, use a tiny dab of clear nail polish to glue the line to the crystal and let it dry for 5 minutes.
  5. Tie the other end of the line to the center of a popsicle stick (or pencil). Adjust the length so that when the stick rests across the top of your jar, the crystal hangs in the center of the solution — not touching the bottom or sides.
  6. Prepare the growing solution: Weigh 30 g of fresh MKP. Heat 100 mL of water to 50 °C. Dissolve the MKP completely (all powder gone, clear solution).
  7. Let the growing solution cool to approximately 30 °C (warm to the touch, not hot). This is critical — if it is too hot it will dissolve your seed crystal.
  8. Pour the cooled growing solution into your empty jar. Gently lower your seed crystal in. Rest the popsicle stick across the rim. The crystal should hang fully submerged and freely in the middle of the solution.
  9. Cover loosely with the coffee filter. Return to your shelf location. Record seed crystal dimensions (length × width × height in mm) and mass if possible.

🏥 Clinical Connection — Seeding & Kidney Stones

  • In the kidney, a tiny crystal fragment or a cellular debris particle can serve as a nucleation site — exactly like your seed crystal. Once a small crystal exists, the supersaturated urine deposits more solute onto it, making it larger.
  • Clinical interventions like increased hydration and citrate supplementation work by reducing urinary supersaturation — the same concept you are exploiting by using a milder solution in Week 2 compared to Week 1.

Week 3 — First Growth Check & Refeed ▶

Goal: Measure crystal growth, remove parasitic crystals, and refresh the supersaturation by adding a refeed solution. This is the most important maintenance step for continued growth over the final two weeks.

  1. Observe your crystal through the glass without disturbing it. Note visible growth, changes in shape, or cloudiness in the solution.
  2. Gently lift the popsicle stick to raise the crystal just above the solution surface. Work quickly — the wet crystal surface can dissolve slightly if left exposed too long (> 30 seconds).
  3. Measure the crystal dimensions (L × W × H) in millimeters with your ruler. Record in your data table.
  4. Place the crystal on a clean paper towel. Loosely cover it with plastic wrap to protect it.
  5. Check for parasitic crystals: Look for small unwanted crystals growing on the fishing line, on the popsicle stick, or on the bottom and sides of the jar. Carefully remove them — they steal dissolved MKP from your growing crystal. Scrape them off with a clean spoon or fingertip.
  6. Prepare the refeed: In a separate beaker, dissolve 10 g of fresh MKP in 30 mL of water heated to 50 °C. Stir until clear.
  7. Let the refeed solution cool to ~30 °C.
  8. Carefully pour the cooled refeed into your growing jar. Stir gently to mix.
  9. Re-suspend your crystal in the refreshed solution. Cover. Return to shelf.
  10. Record the amount of MKP added, the new total volume, and all observations.

💡 What If My Crystal Dissolved?

This means your Week-2 growing solution was unsaturated at room temperature — it could hold more MKP than was present, so it pulled MKP off your crystal. Fix today by making your refeed a little stronger: use 15 g MKP in 30 mL water instead of 10 g, cool to 30 °C, and add. Select a replacement seed from any parasitic crystals that formed on the bottom.

Week 4 — Habit Analysis, Second Refeed & (Optional) Color Band ▶

Goal: Measure growth, analyze the crystal's shape (habit), refeed one more time to drive the final week of growth, and optionally add food coloring to create a visible growth band inside the crystal.

  1. Measure and record crystal dimensions and mass (same protocol as Week 3).
  2. Examine the crystal habit. MKP crystals are tetragonal — an ideal crystal has four equivalent prismatic faces along the long axis and a pointed pyramidal cap on each end. Sketch your crystal and label any faces you can identify.
  3. Note any inclusions (bubbles, cloudiness, or trapped solution inside the crystal). These form when growth is too rapid or the crystal was disturbed during growth.
  4. Remove the crystal from the solution and place it on a clean paper towel. Cover loosely with plastic wrap.
  5. Check for and remove parasitic crystals on the line, popsicle stick, and jar surfaces.
  6. Prepare the second refeed: Dissolve 10 g of fresh MKP in 30 mL of water at 50 °C. Cool to ~30 °C.
  7. Color band experiment (optional): If you have food coloring on hand, add 2–3 drops (any color) to the refeed and stir to distribute evenly. The crystal will incorporate the dye into its new growth layers, creating a visible colored band inside the otherwise clear crystal — direct evidence that the crystal grows layer by layer from the outside.
  8. Pour the refeed into the jar. Re-suspend your crystal. Cover. Return to shelf.
  9. Record all measurements, the color used (if any), and observations.

🏥 Clinical Connection — Growth Zones

  • The colored band in your crystal is analogous to growth lines in kidney stones. When urologists section a kidney stone, they can see concentric growth rings that record changes in urine composition over time — periods of dehydration, dietary changes, or medication adjustments.
  • In pharmacology, growth zones in drug crystals can trap different amounts of impurity, affecting drug purity and dissolution rate.
Week 5 — Final Measurement, Harvest & Data Analysis ▶

Goal: Take final measurements, remove and preserve your crystal, analyze your growth data, and share your result with the class.

  1. Measure final crystal dimensions (L × W × H) in millimeters.
  2. Carefully lift the crystal from the solution. Pat gently with a clean, dry paper towel. Do not rub — you may scratch the crystal faces.
  3. Weigh the dry crystal on the digital scale. Record the final mass.
  4. Carefully cut the fishing line, leaving a small tag if desired.
  5. Preservation: MKP crystals are stable at room temperature but slightly hygroscopic. If your lab is humid, apply a thin, even coat of clear nail polish or clear acrylic spray and let dry — this protects the crystal from absorbing moisture and keeps its surfaces glossy.
  6. Dispose of the remaining solution by pouring down the drain with running water.
  7. Calculate total mass gained: Final crystal mass − Week 2 seed crystal mass = mass deposited from solution. Enter in the Growth Rate calculator on the Data tab.
  8. Calculate average growth rate: Divide total mass gained by the number of days in solution. Express as mg/day or g/week. The calculator does this for you.
  9. Class comparison: Record your final crystal mass and longest dimension on the class data board. Look for patterns:
    • Which shelf locations produced the largest crystals? (Temperature gradients in the room)
    • Did students with a better-formed seed in Week 2 end with a better-formed final crystal?
    • Did colored crystals grow at different rates than uncolored ones?
  10. Complete the reflection questions on the Data tab and submit your final report.

💡 What a Good Result Looks Like

After 3 weeks of growth (from Week 2 seeding to Week 5 harvest), a well-tended MKP crystal typically reaches 1.5–3 cm in its longest dimension and weighs 2–6 g. Crystals that stay clear with sharp faces are more impressive than large but cloudy ones — quality beats size.

Is your crystal growing?
▼
Check: Is the solution clear (no crystals on jar walls)?
▼
YES — clear
▼

NO — rogue crystals
▼
Remove parasitic crystals. They compete with your seed. Clean jar walls and line with a clean finger or spoon.
▼
Is the crystal getting SMALLER?
▼
Solution is undersaturated — it's dissolving your crystal. Add 12 g MKP in 25 mL warm water immediately. Cool to 30 °C before combining.
▼
Crystal is cloudy or cracked?
▼
Growth was too fast or crystal experienced temperature shock. Keep jar away from direct sunlight, AC vents, or heaters. Ensure the cover is on.
▼
When in doubt — ask your instructor!
