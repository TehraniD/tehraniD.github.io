Crystal Growing Project

Monoammonium Phosphate (MAP) — From Solution to Structure

CHM-130 · Fundamentals of Chemistry · 3 Sessions + Harvest · Nov 19 – Dec 15, 2026
Introduction

Over the next four weeks you will grow a crystal you get to keep. On Thursday, November 19 you will make a hot, overloaded solution and let it cool. By the following Tuesday it will have thrown out a crop of tiny crystals. You will choose the best one, pick a color, and hang it in a fresh solution — and from that moment, every day, dissolved molecules will find that seed and lock onto it, face by face, layer by layer. On Tuesday, December 15, right after your final exam, you lift it out: a single crystal, in the color you chose, that did not exist a month ago.

Along the way, solubility, supersaturation, nucleation, and crystal habit stop being vocabulary words and become things you can watch happen in a jar.

The compound is monoammonium phosphate (MAP), formula NH4H2PO4 — the same substance in ABC fire extinguishers and water-soluble fertilizers. It dissolves readily in hot water and crystallizes into tetragonal prisms as the solution cools, by exactly the same mechanism that forms kidney stones in a patient.

🏥 Clinical Connection — Why Nurses Should Understand Crystallization

  • Kidney stones (nephrolithiasis): Calcium oxalate and calcium phosphate crystals form in urine when concentration exceeds solubility — the identical supersaturation mechanism you will drive on purpose in this lab.
  • Gout: Monosodium urate crystallizes in joint fluid when serum uric acid exceeds ~6.8 mg/dL, its solubility limit at 37 °C.
  • Gallstones: Cholesterol crystallization in bile — supersaturation plus nucleation on mucin glycoproteins.
  • Drug polymorphism: The same drug molecule can crystallize into different structures with different dissolution rates. Ritonavir's 1998 market recall happened because an unexpected polymorph appeared during manufacturing and the capsules stopped working.
  • IV crystalloid fluids: "Crystalloid" (Normal Saline, Lactated Ringer's) literally means solutions of substances that can form crystals — as opposed to colloids, which cannot.
Key Concepts

Solubility is the maximum amount of solute that will dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a given temperature. For MAP it rises steeply with temperature — and that steepness is the whole trick:

Temperature (°C)Solubility (g MAP per 100 g H₂O)What It Means for You
1029Cold tap water
20–2537–40Room temperature — where your crystal grows
3046Warm — too warm to add a seed to a 45% solution
4057Warm bath
6083Hot — Session 2 & 3 dissolving temperature
80118Very hot — Session 1 dissolving temperature
100173Boiling water

Supersaturation is what you get when a hot, concentrated solution cools: it now holds more dissolved solute than it can at the new temperature. The excess has to come out as solid. How much excess decides what you get:

  • High supersaturation → many tiny crystals (rapid nucleation everywhere). That is Session 1 — we want a crop of seeds to choose from.
  • Low supersaturation → a few large, clear crystals (slow, orderly growth on an existing surface). That is Sessions 2 and 3 — we want one seed to win.

Nucleation is the moment dissolved molecules first organize into a tiny ordered cluster — the birth of a crystal. It can be homogeneous (spontaneous, in the bulk liquid) or heterogeneous (on a surface: a scratch in the glass, a speck of dust, a fishing line — or the seed you deliberately hang). A mildly supersaturated solution would rather deposit onto your seed than start new crystals from scratch. That preference is what you exploit.

Crystal habit: MAP crystallizes in the tetragonal system (a = b ≠ c, all angles 90°). Expect an elongated four-sided prism with pointed pyramidal ends. Habit tends to persist — a long, needle-like seed grows into a long crystal; a stubby seed grows into a chunkier one. That is one of your customization choices.

⚠️ Safety Precautions

  • Goggles required whenever handling MAP solution or hot water.
  • MAP solutions are mildly acidic (pH ≈ 3.8–4.4) — avoid eye contact. In case of a splash, flush with water for 15 minutes and notify your instructor.
  • Hot water hazard: Session 1 uses near-boiling kettle water. Pour slowly, keep the jar on the bench, and never carry a hot jar across the room.
  • MAP is low-toxicity (it is a food-grade fertilizer ingredient), but do not ingest. Wash hands after each session. Your finished crystal is a decoration, not candy — keep it away from small children and pets.
  • Disposal: Leftover solution goes down the drain with running water; leftover solid in the regular trash. Not hazardous waste.
  • Food coloring stains clothing and countertops — use a few drops, cap the bottle.

Session 1 — Thu 11/19 — Make the Solution & Let It Nucleate ▶

Goal: Dissolve far more MAP in hot water than room-temperature water can hold. As it cools undisturbed over 5 days, the excess crystallizes into a crop of small seed crystals on the bottom of your jar. Time: ~20 minutes.

💡 The Chemistry

At 75–80 °C, 50 mL of water can dissolve about 60 g of MAP. At room temperature it holds only about 19 g. You will dissolve 25 g — comfortably within the hot limit, well above the cold one. As the jar cools, roughly 6 g has nowhere to go but out of solution, and it comes out as dozens of little crystals. By Tuesday you will have a crop to choose from.

  1. Put on goggles. Label your 8 oz jar with your name and section on masking tape. Put the tape near the top so it does not sit in the water bath later.
  2. Place your jar on the scale, tare it, and weigh 25 g of MAP directly into the jar. Record the exact mass.
  3. Measure 50 mL of near-boiling water from the kettle in a graduated cylinder and pour it into the jar, over the powder. The water will be about 75 °C once it is in the jar.
  4. Stir with a glass rod for 1–2 minutes until the solution is perfectly clear — no grains, no haze. (The solution gets noticeably colder as MAP dissolves — dissolving MAP absorbs heat. That is real thermodynamics you can feel through the glass.) If a few grains refuse to dissolve, add 5 mL more hot water and keep stirring.
  5. Take the rod out. From this moment, do not stir, swirl, tap, or move the jar. Every disturbance makes extra crystals nucleate.
  6. Lay a coffee filter over the mouth of the jar and screw the metal ring down over it (no flat lid). Dust stays out; water vapor gets out slowly.
  7. Carry the jar carefully to your assigned shelf spot. It stays there, untouched, until Tuesday 11/24.
  8. In your observation log, record mass of MAP, volume of water, approximate temperature, and the time. Sketch what you see (it should look like plain water).

Session 2 — Tue 11/24 — Pick Your Seed, Pick Your Color, Hang It ▶

Goal: Choose one seed from your crop, rebuild your solution into a mildly supersaturated growing solution in the color you want, and suspend the seed in it. This is the session that decides what your crystal becomes. Time: ~45 minutes, most of it waiting for the solution to cool.

🎨 Make It Yours — decisions you make today

  • Color. Any food color, or none (a water-clear "diamond"). Blue and purple dyes are taken up more weakly than red, green, and yellow, so expect pastel blues. The crystal will always be paler than the solution.
  • Shape. A long, slender seed tends to grow into a long crystal; a short, chunky seed into a chunkier one. Choose the proportions you want to end with.
  • Display. Decide now whether you want to leave a long tail of fishing line to hang it as an ornament (tie a 15 cm loop at the stick end) or keep it in the jar on a bed of your leftover seed crystals.
  • Second jar (optional, bring your own). Grow in the second jar and leave your Session-1 jar as-is with its crop — it becomes the display bed at harvest, and a source of backup seeds if anything goes wrong over the break. Same recipe whatever the jar size; a bigger jar just means a shallower pool, so hang the seed a little lower.
  1. Look at your jar before touching it. Describe and sketch the crop: how many crystals, how big, clear or cloudy, where they formed.
  2. Unscrew the ring, remove the filter. Pour the clear liquid (the "mother liquor") into a beaker and keep it — it is already saturated with MAP and you will reuse it. Pour slowly so the crystals stay in the jar.
  3. Tip the crystals onto a paper towel. Choose your seed:
    • Clear, not milky or cracked
    • Sharp faces and edges — a recognizable four-sided prism
    • About 4–8 mm long — big enough to handle, small enough to have been grown slowly
    • A single crystal, not a fused cluster
    • The shape you want (see Make It Yours)
    Keep the second-best one as a spare. Save the rest of the crop in a labeled cup if you want a display bed at the end.
  4. Attach a 25 cm piece of fishing line to the seed. Easiest: lay the seed on the line, put a tiny dot of clear nail polish where line meets crystal, and let it set 5 minutes while you make the solution. If your seed is big enough, you can instead loop the line twice around its waist and knot it.
  5. Tie the free end to the middle of a popsicle stick. Set the length so that with the stick resting across the jar mouth, the seed hangs about 1 cm above the bottom and clear of the walls. (Your solution will be about 4 cm deep.) If you want an ornament loop, tie it now at the stick end.
  6. Make the growing solution: Into the beaker holding your mother liquor, add 70 mL of hot kettle water and 35 g of fresh MAP. Stir until perfectly clear. The mix will be around 55–60 °C — hot enough to dissolve everything.
  7. Add your color now: 3–5 drops of food coloring, stir once. (More drops does not mean a darker crystal — the crystal takes up only a fraction of the dye.)
  8. Filter it while it is still hot. Fold a coffee filter into a cone, set it in the mouth of your clean jar (your second jar if you brought one — otherwise rinse and dry your Session-1 jar first), and pour the hot solution through it. This catches dust and any undissolved specks — and every speck is a place a stray crystal can start instead of on your seed. Throw the filter away.
  9. Cool it to room temperature — this is the step people rush and regret. Set the jar in the basin of cool tap water and wait, checking with a thermometer. You need 26 °C or below. Why: at 30 °C this solution is still undersaturated and will eat your seed; at 24 °C it is gently supersaturated and will feed it. Do not put the jar in ice — a shock-cooled solution throws out a snowstorm of tiny crystals instead.
  10. Seed-grain test: drop one of your tiniest leftover crystals into the jar and watch it for one minute. If it shrinks or vanishes, the solution is still too warm — keep cooling and test again. If it survives (or grows a little), you are ready.
  11. Lower your seed on its line and rest the stick across the rim. The seed should hang free, fully submerged, not touching anything. (Two-jar students: leave your Session-1 jar and its crop alone — it is your backup seed supply.)
  12. Filter + ring on top. Back to your shelf spot, same place, same orientation. Record seed dimensions (L × W × H in mm) and mass if you could weigh it, color used, and the solution temperature when you added the seed.

🏥 Clinical Connection — Seeding & Kidney Stones

  • In the kidney, a tiny crystal fragment or a bit of cellular debris can serve as a nucleation site — exactly like your seed. Once any crystal exists, supersaturated urine would rather build on it than start fresh.
  • Hydration and citrate therapy work by lowering urinary supersaturation — the same lever you pulled when you made today's solution milder than Session 1's.

Session 3 — Thu 12/10 — Measure, Clean, Refeed (and Optional Second Color) ▶

Goal: After 16 days your crystal has used up most of the excess MAP in its solution and growth has slowed. Today you measure it, clear away the competition, and top up the supersaturation for the final five days. Time: ~30 minutes.

🎨 Make It Yours — today's option

  • Two-tone band. Put a different color in today's refeed and the last five days of growth form an outer layer in the new color around your original core — a visible record that the crystal grew from the outside in. Same color = a uniform crystal. No color = a clear outer shell.
  1. Look before you touch. Is the crystal visibly bigger? Is the solution clear? Are there crystals on the bottom, the walls, the line, or a crust at the waterline? Note everything.
  2. Lift the stick to raise the crystal just out of the solution. Work quickly — keep it out of the air no more than 2 minutes in total. Measure L × W × H in mm. If you can weigh it on the line, do.
  3. Set the crystal (still on its line) on a clean paper towel and lay a second towel loosely over it.
  4. Pour the solution out of the jar into a beaker and keep it. Scrape every stray crystal off the jar bottom and walls and rinse the jar (two-jar students: just swap to your clean other jar). Pick any tiny crystals off the line and the stick with your fingernail. Those "parasites" were eating the MAP your crystal needed.
  5. Make the refeed: In a small beaker, dissolve 12 g MAP in 25 mL hot kettle water. Stir until clear. Add your second color now if you want one (2–3 drops).
  6. Cool the refeed in the water basin to ≤ 26 °C (a small volume cools in about 5 minutes). Pour it into the beaker with your old solution and stir gently.
  7. Seed-grain test again if you have a spare tiny crystal; otherwise check the thermometer reads ≤ 26 °C.
  8. Pour the combined solution back into the clean jar through a fresh coffee filter cone, re-hang your crystal in the center, filter + ring on, back to the shelf.
  9. Record measurements, MAP added, color, and observations.

💡 If something went wrong over the break

Seed dissolved or fell off the line: the bottom of your jar almost certainly has new crystals. Pick the best one, glue it to a fresh line, and continue — five days is enough for a small but real crystal. Crystal is there but a cluster grew on top of it: leave it; a "crystal cluster" is a perfectly good final piece — just refeed and keep going. Big crust at the waterline: evaporation ran ahead of growth; scrape it off, it is just MAP.

Harvest — Tue 12/15 — Right After the Final Exam ▶

Goal: Lift your crystal out for good, dry and seal it, record final numbers, and take it home. Time: ~10–15 minutes.

  1. Lift the stick and let the crystal drip for 10 seconds over the jar.
  2. Lay it on a paper towel and pat dry — never rub. Wet MAP faces scratch easily.
  3. Measure final L × W × H (mm). Weigh it (cut the line first, or weigh with the line and subtract nothing — the line is negligible). Record both in your data table.
  4. Photograph it against a dark background near a window.
  5. Seal (recommended): once fully dry, paint a thin coat of clear nail polish over all faces and let it dry 10 minutes. MAP slowly picks up moisture from humid air; a sealed crystal keeps its glassy surfaces for years.
  6. Finish it your way: cut the line short for a display piece, or leave the loop for an ornament. If you kept your spare crystals, rinse the jar, dry it, add the crystals as a bed, set your crystal on top, lid on — the jar is the gift box.
  7. Pour the leftover solution down the drain with running water.
  8. On the Data tab, run the growth-rate calculator and generate your Crystal Card — print it or screenshot it to go with your crystal.

💡 What a Good Result Looks Like

After 21 days of growth with one refeed, a well-tended MAP crystal in this setup typically reaches 1.5–3 cm in its longest dimension and 2–6 g. Clear faces and sharp edges beat raw size — a 2 cm glass-clear prism is a better crystal than a 3 cm cloudy one.
