Module 1: Cellular Adaptation & Tissue Changes

Learning Objective: Understand how cells adapt to stress through atrophy, hypertrophy, metaplasia, dysplasia, and the role of apoptosis in preventing neoplasia.

Atrophy

1 Question
Q#1 (Exam #72) Multiple Choice

A client on prolonged bed rest exhibits significant muscle weakness and wasting. Which nursing intervention would be MOST effective in preventing further atrophy?

Assisting with active range of motion exercises
Encouraging increased bed rest
Providing a high-calorie diet
Administering sedatives to promote sleep

Key Concept: Disuse Atrophy

Atrophy = decrease in cell SIZE due to lack of use, nutrition, or stimulation. Active ROM exercises maintain muscle tone. More bed rest would WORSEN atrophy. Diet alone won't help without muscle activation.

Remember: USE IT OR LOSE IT - muscles need active stimulation to maintain size.

Metaplasia

3 Questions
Q#2 (Exam #71) Multiple Choice

A 50-year-old with GERD for 3 years has an endoscopy showing histological changes. Why would metaplasia be seen?

Metaplasia results from chronic stomach acid irritation
Metaplasia is a sign that cancer is going to occur
Metaplasia would occur after even one time having GERD
Metaplasia is a sign for abnormal tissue growth

Key Concept: Metaplasia as Adaptation

Metaplasia = REVERSIBLE replacement of one mature cell type with another. In GERD, chronic acid causes esophageal cells to change (Barrett's esophagus). Requires CHRONIC exposure, not a single episode.

Q#69 (Exam #5) Multiple Choice

A client with chronic cigarette smoke exposure develops squamous metaplasia in respiratory epithelium. What is the primary rationale?

Increasing tolerance to toxins in cigarette smoke
Disorganized, uncoordinated, uncontrolled proliferative cell growth
Protecting against infections
Decreasing inflammatory response

Key Concept: Protective Metaplasia

Normal respiratory epithelium (ciliated columnar) changes to squamous (tougher) to resist smoke damage. Trade-off: loses mucociliary clearance.

Q#3 (Exam #39) Multiple Choice

A client with chronic cholecystitis develops intestinal metaplasia in the gallbladder mucosa. What complication is the client at risk for?

Gallbladder cancer
Gallstone formation
Biliary obstruction
Cholecystitis

Key Concept: Metaplasia-Dysplasia-Carcinoma Sequence

Chronic inflammation → Metaplasia → Dysplasia → Carcinoma. Intestinal metaplasia increases cancer risk.

Remember: Normal → Metaplasia → Dysplasia → Carcinoma. Each step increases risk!

Dysplasia

1 Question
Q#70 (Exam #3) Multiple Choice

A client with chronic ulcerative colitis has a colonoscopy revealing dysplasia. What is the nurse's understanding?

It suggests abnormal cell growth with potential for malignancy
It indicates the presence of cancerous cells
It is a benign condition requiring no further intervention
It signifies inflammation of the colon

Key Concept: Dysplasia = Pre-malignant

Dysplasia = DISORGANIZED cell growth with abnormal size, shape, organization. It's PRE-CANCEROUS, not cancer itself. Requires monitoring!

Remember: Dys (bad) + plasia (growth) = abnormal but NOT yet cancer.

Apoptosis & Neoplasia

1 Question
Q#15 (Exam #4) Multiple Choice

Neoplasia could result if cells fail to undergo which regulated process?

Apoptosis
Proliferation
Hypoxia
Necrosis

Key Concept: Apoptosis = Quality Control

Apoptosis = PROGRAMMED cell death (orderly, controlled). Eliminates damaged cells. When it fails → damaged cells survive and multiply → NEOPLASIA.

Remember: Apoptosis is the body's "quality control" - failure leads to tumors!

Module 2: Genetic Disorders

Learning Objective: Understand inheritance patterns and clinical manifestations of sickle cell anemia, cystic fibrosis, Huntington's disease, Down syndrome, and Tay-Sachs disease.

Sickle Cell Anemia

1 Question
Q#1 (Exam #70) Multiple Choice

Which of the following is true regarding sickle cell anemia?

The homozygous form is more severe
Only one parent must be a carrier of the gene
Sickle cell anemia is not a genetic disorder
Only one parent must have sickle cell anemia to pass it on

Key Concept: Autosomal Recessive Inheritance

HbSS (homozygous) = severe disease with sickling crises. HbAS (heterozygous) = sickle cell TRAIT, usually asymptomatic. BOTH parents must be carriers.

Remember: HbSS = Disease. HbAS = Trait. Both parents must contribute the gene!

Which are important treatments for clients with cystic fibrosis?

Chest physiotherapy and airway clearance techniques
Corticosteroid inhalers to reduce inflammation

Key Concept: CF Management

CF causes THICK, STICKY mucus. Treatment: chest PT, bronchodilators, corticosteroids. CF patients need HIGH-FAT, HIGH-CALORIE diets due to malabsorption!

Remember: CF = thick mucus everywhere. They need MORE calories, not fewer!

Huntington's Disease

1 Question
Q#17 (Exam #9) Multiple Choice

What are typical symptoms of Huntington's Disease and how do they progress?

Chorea and involuntary movements, followed by behavioral changes
Muscle weakness and atrophy, followed by seizures
Cognitive decline and memory loss, followed by motor dysfunction
Speech difficulties and swallowing problems, followed by sensory loss

Key Concept: Huntington's Progression

AUTOSOMAL DOMINANT - only need 1 copy. CAG repeat on chromosome 4. CHOREA (dance-like movements) is the hallmark EARLY sign. Onset age 30-50.

Remember: Huntington's HUNTS with CHOREographed movements. Movement problems come FIRST!

Tay-Sachs Disease

1 Question
Q#68 (Exam #6) Multiple Choice

An 18-month-old presents with decline since 6 months, relentless mental/physical deterioration, will become blind, paralyzed, and it is fatal. Diagnosis?

Tay-Sachs
Huntington's disease
Cystic Fibrosis
Down Syndrome

Key Concept: Tay-Sachs Disease

Deficiency of hexosaminidase A. GM2 ganglioside accumulates in neurons. Normal at birth → symptoms at 6 months → death by age 4-5. Cherry-red macula spot is classic finding.

Remember: Normal baby → decline at 6 months → death by 5 years. Cherry-red spot on macula!

Module 3: Infection & Inflammation

Signs of Inflammation

4 Questions
Q#74 (Exam #10) Multiple Choice

Which classic signs indicate an inflammatory process at the site?

Swelling, redness, and heat
Pallor and diaphoresis
Increased heart rate and blood pressure
Nausea and vomiting

Key Concept: Cardinal Signs of Inflammation

5 Cardinal Signs: Rubor (redness), Calor (heat), Tumor (swelling), Dolor (pain), Functio laesa (loss of function).

Remember: Red, Hot, Swollen, Painful, Can't use it = 5 cardinal signs!
Q#5 (Exam #17) Multiple Choice

Which is a phase of acute inflammation?

Vascular permeability
Chronic fibrosis
Cellular exudation
Vasoconstriction

Key Concept: Acute Inflammation Phases

1) Vascular (permeability increases), 2) Cellular (WBCs migrate). Increased permeability → fluid exits → swelling.

To promote leukocyte migration to the site of injury
To increase vascular permeability
To dilate blood vessels
To inhibit the release of inflammatory mediators

Key Concept: Chemotaxis

Chemo (chemical) + taxis (movement) = chemicals guiding WBCs TO the problem area.

Which term is matched INCORRECTLY?

Wound Healing

4 Questions
Q#14 (Exam #18) Multiple Choice

Which is correctly ordered for the four stages of wound healing?

Hemostasis, Inflammation, Proliferation, Wound contraction and remodeling
Inflammation, Proliferation, Hemostasis, Wound contraction and remodeling
Wound contraction and remodeling, Inflammation, Proliferation, Hemostasis
Proliferation, Inflammation, Hemostasis, Wound contraction and remodeling

Key Concept: Wound Healing Phases

1. Hemostasis (stop bleeding), 2. Inflammation (clean up), 3. Proliferation (build new tissue), 4. Remodeling (strengthen scar).

Remember: H-I-P-R: Stop bleeding → Clean → Build → Strengthen!
Q#16 (Exam #29) Multiple Choice

A client with peripheral neuropathy has a heel pressure ulcer covered 100% by eschar. Next action?

Debridement to promote re-epithelialization and new tissue growth
Examination for fragments from traumatic injury
Immediate sterile dressings and surgical evaluation
Consult cosmetic surgery for hyperplasic scar prevention

Key Concept: Eschar Management

Eschar = dead, necrotic tissue. It PREVENTS wound healing. Debridement removes dead tissue to allow healing.

Q#53 (Exam #30) Multiple Choice

Which statement best describes wound dehiscence?

The separation of wound layers due to compromised surgical closure
The process of wound healing with granulation tissue formation
A normal inflammatory response during early healing
Rapid wound closure using sutures or staples

Key Concept: Dehiscence

Dehiscence = wound edges SEPARATE after closure. Risk factors: obesity, infection, poor nutrition, coughing.

Q#54 (Exam #28) Multiple Choice

Which statement best describes secondary intention wound healing?

Healing occurs when wound edges are not closely approximated, with gradual tissue repair and scar formation
Wound edges are closely approximated with minimal scarring
Rapid healing characterized by granulation tissue formation
Wound heals without external intervention using natural processes

Key Concept: Primary vs Secondary Intention

Primary = edges approximated, minimal scar. Secondary = edges NOT approximated, heals from bottom up, larger scar.

Remember: Primary = edges together. Secondary = gap fills from bottom up!

Bacterial Infections

5 Questions
Q#55 (Exam #27) Multiple Choice

A client has increased muscle tone, difficulty relaxing masseter muscles, generalized spasms, and a recent puncture wound. Suspected cause?

Infection by Clostridium tetani
Infection by Clostridium botulinum
Infection by Corynebacterium diphtheriae
Infection by Campylobacter jejuni

Key Concept: Tetanus (Lockjaw)

C. tetani enters through puncture wounds. Produces tetanospasmin → BLOCKS inhibitory neurotransmitters → UNCONTROLLED spasms. Lockjaw (trismus) = classic sign.

Remember: TETanus = TETanic spasms (rigid). BOTulism = floppy (opposite)!
Q#57 (Exam #25) Multiple Choice

This microorganism produces a toxin that prevents muscle contraction, causing paralysis:

Clostridium botulinum
Clostridium tetani
Clostridium difficile
Clostridium perfringens

Key Concept: Botulism

Botulinum toxin BLOCKS acetylcholine release → muscles CAN'T contract → FLACCID paralysis. Sources: improperly canned foods, honey (infants).

Q#58 (Exam #24) Multiple Choice

Which bacterial strain is found in the acronyms MRSA and VRSA?

Staphylococcus aureus
Staphylococcus hemolyticus
Staphylococcus epidermidis
Staphylococcus saprophyticus

Key Concept: Drug-Resistant Staph

MRSA = Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus AUREUS. VRSA = Vancomycin-Resistant. Most pathogenic staph species.

Q#73 (Exam #66) Multiple Choice

For which disease is antibiotics an appropriate first-line therapy?

Bacterial meningitis
Candidiasis
Malaria
Herpes Simplex

Key Concept: Antibiotic Indications

Antibiotics ONLY work against BACTERIA. Candida = fungal, Malaria = parasitic, Herpes = viral.

Remember: Antibiotics = anti-BACTERIA only!
Q#52 (Exam #31) Multiple Choice

What virus causes infectious mononucleosis (mono)?

Epstein-Barr Virus
Herpes Simplex Virus 1
Influenza Virus
Varicella Zoster

Key Concept: Mono (Kissing Disease)

EBV causes mono. Transmitted through saliva. Symptoms: fatigue, fever, pharyngitis, lymphadenopathy. EBV also linked to certain lymphomas!
