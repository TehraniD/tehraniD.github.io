Lab 4 overview - acids, bases, and buffers

The Ballroom Buffer Dancers

Two partners. One trophy. That trophy is the proton (H⁺). They pass it back and forth so the dance floor never floods and never goes empty.

The cast

Acid Antonio
HA - holds the trophy
Base Bella
A⁻ - empty-handed
The Intruder
H⁺ from HCl
The Thief
OH⁻ from NaOH

Why they are "weak" (in a good way)

Strong acids dump every trophy on the floor and walk away. Antonio keeps almost all of his. That leftover stock is the buffer. Same concentration, very different pH:

0.1 M acidWhat happensTypical pH
HCl (strong)Every molecule breaks: HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻~1
CH₃COOH (weak)Almost all stay intact: CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺~2.9

Same 0.1 M. The gap is strength, not concentration. Diluting HCl does not make it weak.

Two directions. One pair.

1. Adding acid - Bella catches the trophy

Key concept: the conjugate base does the work

  • A pushy guest (H⁺ from HCl) storms in waving extra trophies.
  • Bella takes the trophy and becomes Antonio.
  • The dance continues. Free H⁺ barely rises, so pH barely falls.
  • Net: A⁻ + H⁺ → HA

2. Adding base - Antonio hands the trophy over

Key concept: the weak acid does the work

  • A thief (OH⁻ from NaOH) tries to steal trophies.
  • Antonio hands his over. The thief leaves as water (H₂O).
  • The dance continues. Free OH⁻ is gone, so pH barely rises.
  • Net: HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O

Beakers 1 and 2 are the control. Every "small" number only means something compared with those two. Acetate defends ~4.7. Ammonia defends ~9.3. Both resist. There is no pH-7 rule.

Memory Hook

A buffer is a dance pair, one trophy apart. Acid arrives - Bella takes it. Base arrives - Antonio gives it. The floor never floods and never goes empty, until the partners run out.
