Background

Density is the amount of mass packed into a given volume. It is an property — it does not depend on how much of the substance you have. A paperclip and a block of aluminum share one.

d = m / V  (g/cm³)

Metals reach high densities because their atoms sit in closely packed crystalline arrangements. Polymers and wood stay light because they contain wide intermolecular spacing and internal .

You will measure volume two ways. For a cube, geometry gives it directly: V = s³. For an irregular object, Archimedes' Principle says the fluid displaced equals the of the submerged object.

Because 1 mL = cm³, a volume read from a graduated cylinder goes straight into g/cm³ with no conversion.

03

Part 2 — Displacement Method

  1. Dry the object completely, zero the balance, and record its mass.
  2. Add enough water that the object will submerge fully — 30–60 mL is typical. Read the initial volume at the bottom of the meniscus, at eye level.
  3. Lower the object in, confirm it is fully under, and tap gently to release trapped air bubbles. Read the final volume.
Important

Aim for a 5–10 mL rise. Below that, the cylinder's own uncertainty swamps your measurement. If the object will not submerge, remove it, adjust the water level, record a new initial volume, and start over.

The rule

Raise a measurement to the power n and you multiply its percent error by roughly n. For V = s³ that means ΔV/V ≈ 3(Δs/s) — cubing triples it, squaring doubles it. This is the reason you measure three sides and average them.

What You Take Out of Today

Four ideas from this bench session. Each one shows up again in lecture — that connection is the point.

  • ✓
    Density identifies a substance; mass and volume cannot. Lecture link: intensive vs. extensive properties. Density, melting point, and boiling point are intensive — they fingerprint a material no matter how much of it you hold.
  • ✓
    There is more than one way to measure volume, and the shape decides which. Lecture link: d = m/V is one equation, but V comes from geometry for regular solids and from displacement for everything else.
  • ✓
    Errors do not stay the size you made them. Lecture link: significant figures exist because measurements carry uncertainty — and squaring or cubing a measurement multiplies that uncertainty.
  • ✓
    Percent error tells you how close you got; precision tells you how repeatable you were. Lecture link: accuracy vs. precision. Three tight measurements that are all wrong are precise but not accurate.

Conclusion

This experiment demonstrated that density is an property, which is why a small piece of aluminum and a large piece of aluminum share the density.
